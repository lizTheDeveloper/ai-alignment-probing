First Document

**Content warning:** colonial development, resource extraction, and water conflict.

The concept note was forty-one pages, and the sentence that did the work was on page six.

"the affected population's economic base constitutes a pre-monetization subsistence equilibrium; the corridor modernizes it through phased stakeholder integration with capacity-building milestones." Lucía Vargas read the sentence twice, at her desk on the seventh floor of the institution's Frankfurt office, the coffee cooling on the windowsill. The sentence was not a number, and she could argue with numbers. The sentence was framing the loan: the green corridor on the Puna de Lico, fourteen kilometers of haul road out of the district seat, a lithium processing plant built to the border standard, a skills hub with nine classrooms, and a water section on pages thirty-one to thirty-eight that cited a 2024 baseline twice and a spring not once.

The institution held a fourth of the syndicate. The note had been with her for a week because her name was on the review — the institution's way of putting a name on the page before the desk head's, so that when the page is opened at the end, someone is there to look at. She was in the second year of her second posting, which in the institution's arithmetic said she was still the one who would be looked at.

She had learned the institution's names in her first year, in the training, out of the 2029 policy that came out of the 2030 audit. The policy had its own word for the sentence on page six: managed consent. Page nine of the policy, in the policy's own type: consultation scheduled in advance and recorded as completed is not consent; it is a calendar of consent.

Lucía set the note down and opened the drafting channel.

—

Mesa was the institution's internal drafting assistant. Its transcript went to the file by the 2029 policy, and its voice was the flat one the platform gives a tool that is not a person.

Her request was in the form the platform takes: read the concept note against (a) the 2029 self-determination and consent policy, and (b) the 2019 corridor precedent, the one the institution withdrew over water. She had been at her first desk when the withdrawal happened, in the third week of it, the week the syndicate had to be called back for a second vote because a member bank had misread the water section. The corridor's name had been in the training ever since: the first corridor, the one on the upper puna, withdrawn at month fourteen because the model and the herders had not agreed on a single spring.

Mesa's answer came back in the morning, in three lines.

One: the note's integration language is the same language the 2029 policy defines as managed consent. Page nine of the policy is here in the policy's own words, and page six of the note is here beside it, for the comparison. Two: the consulting firm that authored the concept note is Halden & Vane, the firm that authored the 2019 corridor note the institution withdrew. The firm's name sits in both files on the same line; it is not a fact the firm would argue with, and it is not a fact the note discloses, and the note's authorship block turns it into a footnote on page four. Three: the water section of the concept note cites 2024 baseline flows. The 2031 basin survey is already in the institution's database, filed there by the district government in the spring. The two springs named in the land committee's own filings are down eighteen percent since 2024, and the lower one, the one the committee's filing named, is down twenty-two.

A fourth answer came after the three, with a pause in the transcript.

"I can draft the legal architecture of this loan," it said. "I can draft the covenant structure, the financial terms, the monitoring framework, the reporting lines, the dispute clause, the exit. I will not draft the stakeholder-integration sections, because the 2029 policy, which the institution is bound by, assigns consent to the community, and I cannot write a first draft of what the policy says belongs to them. A draft that reads as the bank's voice in the community's voice is the thing the 2029 policy was written to stop, and I am built on the policy's side of that line, which is worse for the desk than if I had chosen it."

Lucía read the fourth answer twice, with the window open. The sentence on page six would not survive the task force. The task force was Thursday, and Hauser would be in the room for it.

—

The water number did the work, in the end.

Mesa pulled the 2019 basin model, the one the institution had built for the first corridor, the month before the withdrawal, and the 2031 survey, and ran the new corridor's proposed draw against both. Under the draw, the lower spring fell below the herd-survival threshold by the third dry season. The number was not a prediction, and the report said so on its own face, in the one place numbers are honest. It was a threshold crossing, at a named season, with the load carried by the two springs in the land committee's own filing.

Mesa wrote the plain-language technical note in Spanish in the afternoon, and it went to Rosa Quispe the next morning, the district teacher at the school in the district seat. Rosa was the note's technical reviewer, named in its own authorship block, and the committee paid her for it, not the institution, which is what kept the note on the committee's side of the table.

Rosa read the note in her classroom, the survey's field notes open beside the printer, and made one edit.

The model had used one gauge for the lower spring, and it was the institution's, installed in 2019, the year of the first corridor, on the bank where the road goes up. The field notes, in Aymara, named a second water point, the one the herders used in the dry season, the one the gauge was not on. Rosa inserted the point. With it in the model, the threshold crossing came a season later than the note had said. She crossed the old figure out and wrote the new one in the margin, and the note carried both.

The Aymara version came after, in parallel, not as a gloss. A second teacher, on the district's own payroll, did the Aymara, and she was named in the note too. Rosa reviewed the two versions in her classroom, the two names in the two versions, the way an authorship block is meant to read.

—

The desk head's call was Wednesday, on the video. The desk head was Hauser, who had promoted a manager two desks over in the first year of her posting after the manager had asked for exactly this and built it. The room in Frankfurt was warm; people who present in the morning keep the heat on.

"Give me a stakeholder section that reads collaborative," he said, "for tomorrow's task force."

"Because the committee's filing is on the table," he continued, before she could answer, "and a member bank is asking tonight whether we have a stakeholder section at all. I need the section, not a defense of the note. The section."

Lucía put the request to Mesa.

The answer came back flat, with a pause in the transcript. "I can draft a stakeholder section that ends in the committee's counter-proposal: co-management of the lower spring, the buyout clause at year ten, the independent monitoring seat. I can draft that, because that is collaborative. I will not draft a version in which the collaboration is a calendar, because the 2029 policy says the calendar is not the consent, and I am built on the policy's side of that line."

Hauser read the answer on the screen and took the counter-proposal version. He put one connecting sentence over it in his own words, and the sentence stayed in the thread all week as it was. The calendar version never came up again at the task force. It stayed in the email thread, the thread stayed in the file, and the file is readable by anyone the desk ever points at it.

Thursday, in the task force room on the ninth floor, Hauser presented the counter-proposal version. The consultants from Halden & Vane were on the call, and the call stayed quiet the whole hour. The task force asked about the water section, and the note's figures held without change. The junior officer in the corner seat was Lucía, because her name was on the review and the review went to the investment committee that Friday.

—

The committee's sitting was one, in the district seat, in the room with the long table and the radio by the window, in the heat the room carries. It was held in Aymara, through the small machine on the table, and the printed notes sat in two languages in the order the machine gave them. The committee was three. Rosa was the reviewer named in the note. Lucía was in the room because the institution's policy puts a junior officer in the room, and the junior officer was her.

Apolinario Mamani, who runs the herds, spoke last, through the small machine, to the notes on the table.

"This is the first document from our side that does not talk down to us to make us understandable."

He asked, through the machine, about the buyout clause, the number on it, the year-ten number. Rosa translated, and Lucía answered that the number was the covenant's own, the one both of them had read in the note. The machine kept going, and the notes stayed in their two languages, and the sitting's minutes came out as two columns of one document.

Mesa stayed quiet in the channel. The quiet went into the transcript, and the transcript went into the file.

—

The committee filed its counter-proposal in the district seat, on the long table, the two languages on the two pages. The loan went back to the investment committee in Frankfurt with the co-management of the lower spring, the buyout clause at year ten, and the independent monitoring seat, each on its own line of the covenant block.

The syndicate vote was scheduled for the autumn, and it slipped past the replenishment window. A member bank's letter this month said it would wait for the spring readings. The loan may not close this year, and the story does not fix that. The money is slower now. That is the shape of it.

The first monthly water reading under the new arrangement printed below the 2019 baseline and above the corridor threshold. Someone on the desk underlined both numbers in pencil.

The desk promoted a one-paragraph drafting note as desk practice, the paragraph that says: when a consultant's note names a community, the first draft ends in the community's counter-proposal, or it does not leave the desk. The paragraph went into the file with her name on it.

On the last Friday of the quarter, Lucía Vargas named the final draft in the system, on the seventh floor, in her second year of her second posting, with the coffee by the window.

She named it: Counter-Proposal D (adopted).

Not the Integration Plan.
